# Visual, Subtitle, Shot, and Asset Guide

## Visual principle

Every important visual should perform at least one function:

- prove the scene/event
- identify a person/place/object
- explain geography
- show scale
- explain a number
- show a timeline
- support history
- expose a rule/document
- provide a visual transition

Do not use random stock footage merely to keep the screen moving.

## Shot-unit logic

Do not cut mechanically on every sentence.

Treat one **information unit** as one visual unit. A typical unit may last 3–8 seconds, but meaning determines timing.

Possible visual changes:
- replace footage/photo
- crop or zoom
- map push-in
- timeline advance
- key phrase card
- number animation
- document highlight
- person switch
- before/after comparison
- diagram

## Suggested shot entry

For each timed block include:

- `时间码`
- `口播`
- `画面`
- `屏幕大字`
- `素材建议`

Do not force every field to be long. Keep execution readable.

## Subtitle strategy

Subtitles are not merely verbatim transcripts.

Extract high-value visual text:
- names
- places
- dates
- one strong number
- key rule
- reversal phrase
- core conclusion
- short memorable analogy

Prefer short units such as `8000倍申购` over full explanatory sentences when the narration already carries context.

## Narration vs screen

Let the screen carry precision when useful.

Example:
- screen: `2026.07.30`
- narration: `真正失控，是在月底。`

Example:
- screen: `+96.8%`
- narration: `差不多翻了一倍。`

Use exact spoken numbers when legal/financial/scientific precision itself matters.

## Asset categories

### Must-have
Assets without which the viewer cannot understand or trust the story:
- official announcement/judgment
- map for critical geography
- key person/product/event image
- crucial data visual

### Recommended
Assets that significantly improve pace or comprehension:
- secondary actors
- historical photos
- comparison graphics
- related archival footage

### Alternatives
When direct real footage is unavailable:
- maps
- self-generated charts
- timelines
- diagrams
- clearly labeled concept/AI illustrations

## Research and provenance

When source article visuals are insufficient, search for:
- official images/video
- government/institutional material
- maps
- person/product/company images
- historical archives
- public-domain or clearly licensed images
- documents and court/government records

Keep source metadata for the production team.

Never present AI-generated material as genuine news footage. Label AI visuals as `AI生成示意图` or equivalent when used.

## Copyright awareness

Prefer official/public-domain/clearly licensed material. For news media images or clips, record the original publisher/source so the editor can make a rights decision before publication. Do not imply that public web availability equals unrestricted reuse permission.
