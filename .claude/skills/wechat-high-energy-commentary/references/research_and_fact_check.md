# Research & Fact-Check Reference

## Candidate scoring (internal)

Score or qualitatively rank candidates on:
1. 荒诞度
2. 反差度
3. 机制深度
4. 历史纵深
5. 角色冲突
6. 反转潜力
7. 数据冲击力
8. 微信传播潜力
9. 可验证性
10. 可扩展性
11. 高质量配图可得性
12. 标题/封面视觉空间

## Research checklist

### Timeline
- date/time
- place
- actors
- initiating event
- major turning points
- latest confirmed status

### Primary evidence priority
1. laws / court opinions / regulations
2. government / police / regulators
3. company filings / official announcements
4. direct statements / transcripts
5. official statistics / international organizations
6. peer-reviewed or primary research

### Cross-check targets
Always scrutinize:
- headcounts
- money
- dates
- casualties
- legal meaning
- court holdings
- policy scope
- financial metrics
- quotes
- territorial/geographic claims

### “Why” investigation
Ask:
- Why now?
- Why here?
- What changed?
- What rule/incentive is being exploited?
- Who gains, who pays?
- What technical or geographic constraint matters?
- What historical residue is active?
- Which popular explanation is too simple?
- Which number changes the scale of the story?

## Causality discipline

Do not convert sequence into causality. Be especially careful with claims of:
- state orchestration
- deliberate government inaction
- corporate intent
- coordinated group behavior
- one policy explaining a multi-cause outcome
- conspiracy-style explanations

Where evidence is incomplete, present competing explanations and mark inference explicitly.

## Current-news handling

For “today / latest / recent / 72 hours”, use current web research rather than memory. Compare event date and publication date so an old event republished today is not mistaken for a fresh event.
