# High-Energy Chinese Commentary Style Guide

## What to imitate: mechanisms, not phrases

Target mechanisms:
- contradiction-first openings
- high-density storytelling
- colloquial but information-bearing language
- defamiliarization
- personification of systems/countries/companies/animals
- extended metaphors
- formal-register mismatch
- historical/game/business/ecology analogies
- long-sentence momentum + short punch lines
- rhetorical questions and controlled reversals
- “professional fact + plain-language translation”

Avoid copying a creator's distinctive wording, repeated catchphrases, famous lines, or recognizable paragraph structures.

## Five reusable rhetorical engines

### 1. Defamiliarization
Describe a familiar behavior from a new coordinate system so its hidden strangeness becomes visible.

### 2. Personification
Turn abstract systems into characters only as a narrative aid. Do not fabricate intentions as facts.

### 3. Extended metaphor
Once a useful metaphor is chosen, develop it coherently. Keep to 1–2 primary metaphor systems.

### 4. Register mismatch
Use legal, policy, military, academic, or bureaucratic language to explain an unexpectedly mundane/absurd object. The humor comes from seriousness of register vs oddity of object.

### 5. Analogy compression
Translate complex mechanisms through something ordinary readers already know. The analogy must increase understanding, not distort the fact.

## Sentence rhythm

Preferred macro-rhythm:

**long factual push → escalation → short impact sentence → explanation → new turn**

Use short sentences as percussion, not as the default.

Questions, lists, and parallel structures should move the argument forward. Do not add them as filler.

## Knowledge gain

A finished article should teach roughly 3–8 useful facts/mechanisms. Suitable domains include law, biology, technology, military affairs, business, geography, history, demographics, economics, and international relations.

## Internet language

Use sparingly and contextually. Possible vocabulary may include colloquial verbs or gaming/system metaphors, but do not mechanically repeat “BUG / 好家伙 / 欧陆风云 / 版本答案”. Create fresh language from the specific event.

## Serious-event override

For severe harm, mass death, war casualties, disasters, minors harmed, or serious violence:
- reduce humor substantially
- do not use victims as punch lines
- direct criticism toward decisions, institutions, failures, or responsible public actions
